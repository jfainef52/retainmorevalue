Repositioned Plan — Distribution Years
Required distributions are modelled on top of taxable Social Security; 85% of benefits are treated as includible, since the provisional-income thresholds governing that inclusion ($32,000 / $44,000 joint) have never been indexed. Medicare IRMAA surcharges are shown as a separate layer because they are a cost that required distributions cause rather than a tax upon them. Conservative methodology. Two assumptions deliberately favour the status quo: required minimum distributions are taxed as though they were the client’s only ordinary income, filling the lowest available brackets and minimising tax on the do-nothing path; and the qualified plan distribution funding the ETA structure is taxed at the highest marginal bracket. Any distortion understates the advantage shown for repositioning rather than overstating it. Hypothetical illustration, not a guarantee of future results or tax outcomes. Qualified plan figures reflect the median of 10,000 bootstrapped simulations; half of outcomes finish better and half worse. Policy values are taken from the carrier illustration as prepared and are not guaranteed. Federal estate tax exemption reflects current law under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act ($15M individual / $30M married, effective for deaths on or after January 1, 2026, indexed for inflation) and is subject to future legislative change. Gifts to an irrevocable trust reduce the exemption available at death dollar for dollar. Death benefit is excluded from the gross estate only where the policy is owned by a properly structured irrevocable trust with an independent trustee and no obligation to satisfy estate liabilities; a policy transferred into trust rather than issued to it is subject to the three-year lookback under IRC 2035. The income in respect of a decedent deduction under IRC 691(c) is not modeled. Not tax or legal advice — consult qualified tax counsel and an estate planning attorney.
